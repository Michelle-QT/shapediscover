ShapeDiscover: cover-learning benchmark results

Benchmark harness + R&D since the benchmarking effort began. Generated 2026-06-21; every table is computed directly from committed CSVs in benchmarks/results/ (no hand-entered numbers).

ShapeDiscover learns a fuzzy cover whose nerve should recover the topology of a point cloud. Headline metric: the homology-recovery quotient (fraction of the nerve filtration with the exactly-correct Betti vector). Over-optimization (the recent focus): at fixed data and n_cover, with early stopping off, recovery rises to an interior-iteration peak then declines; severity = (peak − final) / peak.

11. Future directions

  • Anti-element-death / loss-balance lever (best-grounded candidate): a per-element measure floor or a higher default measure weight. Evidence: measure=4 removes the donut's over-optimization. Untested: generalization to Klein / RP² / raw anisotropic torus, cost on the simple manifolds, sampling-invariance. The default algorithm is unchanged.
  • Whitening for linearly anisotropic data: validated on the flat torus; does not fix the donut's nonlinear/local anisotropy.
  • High intrinsic-dimensional homology: gated by the dense-nerve blow-up; needs genuinely sparse covers before n_cover can be pushed higher (sparsemax shrinks the nerve but breaks the tori).
  • Persistence-based clustering: replace the single-threshold components route that under-segments real data.
  • Graph / cover trade-off: no single graph or cover map wins across simple-vs-hard topology; data-adaptive selection is the open lever.
